# Evidence for the problem

**Funza Teacher Studio · v0.1 · 24 November 2026**

At the work-in-progress demo on 24 November, our instructor asked us not to assume that teachers are overworked, because research on it exists. This document collects that research, says how strong each source is, and says what we still have to ask teachers.

## 1. What we can say

1. **Saskatchewan educators plan and mark outside the school day.** In a survey of one Saskatchewan division they reported 23.2 hours of work a week outside the school day: 7.1 on planning and 4.8 on assessment [1].
2. **Marking is a leading source of stress.** In Alberta, 52% of teachers named too much marking as a stressor, the most of any stressor listed. Teachers worked 47 hours a week [2, 3].
3. **Teachers adapt materials more than they start from nothing.** In a national U.S. survey, 45% combined several materials and 25% mostly used their own. Only 30% used a single curriculum [4].
4. **Divisions have reason to ask for little student data, kept briefly.** A 2024 breach at a software vendor reached 28,635 student records in one Saskatchewan division. Over-collection, data that was never deleted and no multi-factor sign-in were among the root causes [5, 6].

No source here covers only Saskatchewan Grade 10–12 teachers. Interviews are still needed for that.

## 6. What this changes

1. **Lead with adapting.** "Bring what you already teach with" is better supported than "generate a course". The plan is in [Modify my existing material](../04_development/07_modify_existing_material.md).
2. **Marking deserves more weight.** It is the top stressor in Alberta, and Funza marks only Live answers today.
3. **Don't say high school teachers are the most overwhelmed.** Pew found the opposite in the U.S.

Ask in interviews: hours spent planning and marking in a normal week; what they reuse from last year and what they change; which file formats they keep; who approves a new tool, and how.

## 7. Worth reading, not yet used

We could not open these, or saw only a search summary. Read them before citing them.

- RAND, [Teachers' Use of Instructional Materials from 2019–2024](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA134-30.html). A search summary says high school teachers are more likely than others to modify materials. **This would be the most useful one to confirm.**
- Fraser Institute, [full survey of Grade 6–12 teachers](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/survey-of-canadian-grades-6-12-teachers-on-ai-use.pdf). A search summary says 67.7% use AI at work and that lesson preparation is the top use.
- Saskatchewan [Task Force on Teacher Time, Final Report](https://stf.sk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/task_force_teacher_time_final_report.pdf), January 2016. We searched its text for weekly hours and found none.
