Teacher Studio flows and features

How a Saskatchewan Grade 10–12 teacher moves through Funza, step by step, in its two modes: Live, for questions students answer on their phones during class, and Prepare, for courses, notes, quizzes and exams that students download. Screens and the backend come after you sign off on this.

Your sketch, read back

This is how I read the photo and your notes. If a line is wrong, the rest of the plan inherits the mistake, so check these first.

Live

  • Upload notes from the computer, or use an existing course and its notes.
  • Funza picks questions from that topic. The teacher chooses how many and sets a timer.
  • Students scan the QR code and enter their name or student number. Then they can see the questions.
  • When the timer ends the teacher clicks Reveal, and everybody sees the answers.
  • Funza corrects the answers and shows the stats: which questions most students got wrong.

Not live (Prepare)

  • Add or create a course; view its curriculum and chapters; add more courses.
  • Create a quiz: generate it, customize it, or upload a past quiz so the new one looks like it with only the chapters changed.
  • Create an exam: a longer version of the quiz (merge with quiz, or keep separate).
  • Create notes and slides, and tell a chatbot what to add.
  • Everything goes through review; the teacher approves and publishes.
  • Students get it by scanning a QR code, visiting a website, or through Google Classroom.

The whole map

From sign-up to the two modes, and where students touch the product. The dashed arrows are the two places the modes feed each other.

First-time setup (once)

  1. Sign up with Google or email. Name and school (optional).
  2. Grades you teach: 10, 11, 12. Subjects.
  3. Pick your courses from the Saskatchewan curriculum (for example Chemistry 30). Each gets chapters from the Ministry units: Chemical Bonding and Materials Science, Chemical Equilibria, Electrochemistry.
  4. Optional: connect Google Classroom and link each Funza course to a Classroom class.
  5. Optional: drop in notes you already have, per chapter.

Home

  • Go live and Make something: the two big buttons.
  • Your courses, each with its next chapter.
  • “Waiting for review”: one count, opens the review queue.
  • Last live session: score and the most-missed question, with “Make practice”.
  • Settings: profile, Classroom, AI usage this month, student data.

Live mode: a timed question set in class

The teacher runs it from a laptop connected to the projector. Students use their phones. The top row shows the session’s state as it moves along.

  1. L1
    Go live Teacher

    From Home, or from “Run live” on any approved quiz in a course.

  2. L2
    Choose a source Teacher

    Upload a PDF or notes from the computer, pick a course chapter (its saved notes are used), or start from a quiz already in the course.

  3. L3
    Funza suggests questions Funza

    About 15 questions from the source, in four phone-friendly types: multiple choice, true or false, a number, short answer. Each shows the page or section it came from.

  4. L4
    Pick and tune Teacher

    Tick the ones to keep (how many is your choice), edit wording, ask for “easier”, “harder” or “3 more”. Set the timer (default 5 minutes for the whole set). Choose what students type: name, and student number on or off.

  5. L5
    Open the lobby Teacher

    The projector shows a big QR code, a 6-character code (for example K7QM4P) and the web address. Names appear as students join. The teacher can remove a name (a joke name, a duplicate).

  6. L6
    Join Student

    Scan the QR or type the code, enter name and student number, see “You’re in. Wait for your teacher.” No app, no account.

  7. L7
    Start and answer Teacher Student

    The teacher presses Start; one clock starts for everyone and the questions appear on every phone. Answers save as students go. A student who joins late gets the time that is left. A phone that drops reconnects with its answers intact. The teacher sees “21 of 28 answered” and can end early.

  8. L8
    Time’s up, Funza marks Funza Teacher

    Answers lock. Multiple choice, true or false and numbers are checked exactly by code. Short answers get a mark from Funza (correct, partly, incorrect). The teacher can flip any short-answer mark before revealing.

  9. L9
    Reveal Teacher Student

    The projector shows each question, its correct answer and the share of the class that got it. Each phone shows that student’s answers next to the correct ones and their score. Names are never projected unless the teacher turns that on.

  10. L10
    Results Teacher Funza

    Per question: percent correct and the most-picked wrong answer. Per student: score. Per chapter or outcome: how the class did. Funza writes two or three lines on what most students missed and why. Each question’s percent correct also updates its difficulty in the chapter’s question bank. Actions: save to the course, export CSV, Make practice on the weak questions (opens Prepare with a draft ready to review), run it again later.

TimerDefault: one shared clock that the teacher starts. Your note described a clock per student that starts when they join; that is question 1 below.
PacingDefault: the whole set at once, students scroll through it. A later option: one question at a time, the teacher moves the class on.
Session statesSet up, lobby, answering, marking, revealed, closed. A closed session stays in the course with its results.
Exit ticketA preset: 3 questions from today’s chapter, 3 minutes. Same flow, two clicks.

Prepare mode: courses and materials

Everything a teacher makes sits in a course chapter and moves through the same four states before students see it.

  1. P1
    Add a course Teacher

    Pick grade and subject from the Saskatchewan curriculum; chapters fill in from the Ministry units. Or make a custom course. Rename, reorder, add or hide chapters. “View curriculum” shows each chapter’s official outcomes and indicators, quoted with their source.

  2. P2
    Chapter page Teacher

    Four lists: Sources (your notes, PDFs, past exams, kept here for reuse), Materials (notes, quizzes, exams, slides with their state), Question bank (every approved question from this chapter), Live sessions (past results).

  3. P3
    Make Teacher

    One box: “What do you want to make for Chemical Equilibria?” plus chips (see Cards to quick prompts). Typing works too: “a 20-minute quiz on Le Chatelier, harder than last time”.

  4. P4
    Quiz, test or exam from a template Teacher Funza

    One builder. Pick a template (the built-in ones in the table below, or your own), pick the chapters, and Funza fills it: questions from the chapters’ banks first, new ones only for what is missing. Question mix, marks, time, difficulty, order, worked solutions and rubric stay in collapsed rows under “Adjust”.

  5. P5
    Make a template Teacher Funza

    Three ways: describe the format in plain words, upload a past exam for Funza to read, or set the controls and press “Save as template”. New exams then follow that format with only the chapters changing. Details in Exam templates.

  6. P6
    Notes and slides Teacher Funza

    Style chips: Full notes, Summary, Worked examples, Simplified, Revision sheet, Slides. Then an editor with Funza’s chat beside it: “add a real-life example to part 2”. Quick prompts above the chat: Simplify, Add examples, Add check-your-understanding questions, Add a key terms box, Make it shorter.

  7. P7
    Review Teacher

    Every part Funza wrote stays marked until the teacher checks it. Automatic checks: calculation answers recomputed, marks add up, every chosen outcome covered. Redo one question (easier, harder, different context) or use the chat for bigger changes. “Send for approval” moves it to in review.

  8. P8
    Approve Teacher

    Locks version 1, and its questions join the chapter’s question bank. Editing later makes version 2; students keep seeing version 1 until version 2 is approved.

  9. P9
    Publish Teacher

    Pick where it goes, any mix: Student page (shows in the course page behind the course QR and link), Google Classroom (material or assignment, draft, now or scheduled), Print or PDF. Answer keys stay with the teacher; “Release the answer key” is a separate, later click.

Templates for exams and notes

A template is a format without its content. For an exam: sections, question types, how many of each, marks, time, difficulty mix, instructions, cover page, wording style and any pinned questions. For notes: the layout and the parts every handout has. The teacher makes one once and reuses it for every chapter and every term.

  1. T1
    Describe it Teacher Funza

    Type the format in plain words: “Section A: 20 multiple choice, 1 mark each. Section B: 5 short answers, 4 marks each. Section C: 2 long answers, 10 marks each. 90 minutes. Formula sheet attached.” Funza turns it into a template and shows it as an empty skeleton (sections, counts, marks) for a yes before writing any question.

  2. T2
    Upload a past exam Teacher Funza

    Drop last year’s exam as a PDF, Word or PowerPoint file (scanned pages come later), or pick one of your Funza materials. Funza reads its structure and its style: how questions are worded, how marks are shown, what the instructions say. None of its questions are copied, and the file is read once and not kept: only the format is saved.

  3. T3
    Set the controls Teacher

    The controls from today’s Create screen, grouped into four collapsed rows: Sections and questions, Marks and time, Difficulty, Extras (answer key, worked solutions, rubric, versions A and B). Closed, each row shows its value in one line, for example “3 sections · 27 questions · 60 marks”, so the whole setup reads at a glance.

  4. T4
    Pin questions Teacher

    Pin any question to the template, such as a favourite long answer or the data question you always ask. Every exam built from that template includes it unchanged, and Funza fits the rest around it.

  5. T5
    Save it Teacher

    Name it (“Chem 30 unit test”) and save. Your templates sit beside the built-in ones; any of them can be duplicated and changed.

  6. T6
    Use it Teacher Funza

    Make, Exam, choose the template, pick the chapters (Chemical Equilibria, Electrochemistry). Funza fills the format from the chapters’ question banks first and writes new questions only for the gaps, from those chapters’ outcomes and your notes. Then review, approve and publish as usual.

  7. T7
    Improve it Teacher

    If you change the structure during review (one more question in Section B, different marks), Funza asks “Save this to the template?”

  8. T8
    Templates for notes Teacher Funza

    The same three ways make a notes template: describe it (“Cornell notes, a key terms box, three check-your-understanding questions at the end”), upload the handout you already use, or set the style controls and save. Notes for any chapter then come out in that layout.

One direction per screenMake shows three choices: what you are making, which template, which chapters. Everything else is collapsed.

Skeleton firstFunza shows the empty structure before writing questions, so a wrong format costs one click, not a whole draft.

Question bank per chapter

Every question the teacher approves is kept with its chapter. The bank grows on its own as the teacher works, and it is the first place Funza looks when it builds anything new.

  1. B1
    Questions join the bank on approval Teacher

    When a quiz, test or exam is approved, each question is saved to its chapter with its type, marks, outcome, answer and marking notes. Questions from an uploaded past exam are not added unless the teacher chooses to.

  2. B2
    Bank first, then Funza Funza

    A new draft takes questions from the chapters’ banks that fit the template, and Funza writes only what is missing. The summary line says it: “27 questions: 12 from your bank, 15 new”. Cheaper and faster, and the reused questions are ones the teacher already trusts.

  3. B3
    Live results set difficulty Funza

    After each live session, a question’s percent correct updates its difficulty. For example: under 40% correct is hard, over 80% is easy, with a note of how many students it is based on.

  4. B4
    Browse and manage Teacher

    On the chapter page: search, filter by type, outcome or difficulty, see how often and when each question was used, edit, retire, or pin it to a template. A question can also go straight into a live session.

No repeats by accidentA question used with the same course recently is skipped unless it is pinned (see question 10).
Starter contentToday’s hand-written Science 10 bank (two Force and Motion outcomes) becomes the first entries for that course.
Answer keys stay privateThe bank is the teacher’s only. Students never see it, and published copies never include answers.

What a student sees

Two pages, both on the phone, neither needs an account.

  1. S1
    Live answer page

    Scan the QR (or open the address and type the code), enter name and student number, wait in the lobby, answer before the clock hits 0:00, then see the correct answers and their own score when the teacher reveals.

  2. S2
    Course page

    Scan the course QR poster, open the link the teacher shared, or open the Classroom post. See the course’s chapters and what the teacher published in each, then view it or download a PDF. Only published material appears; drafts and answer keys never do.

Suggestions

Things you did not mention that I think a teacher would expect. All are in the flows above unless noted. The question bank, pinned questions, difficulty from live results and notes templates were approved on 19 November and are now part of the plan.

Live

  1. Run any approved quiz live. One button connects the two modes.
  2. A code as well as the QR. Phones at the back of the room often cannot scan a projector.
  3. Projector view separate from your controls. The class sees the QR, clock and answers; you see names and marks.
  4. Check Funza’s marks before the reveal. Its short-answer marks are suggestions.
  5. Exit ticket preset. 3 questions, 3 minutes, from today’s chapter.
  6. Leaderboard, off by default. Optional for review games; low stakes by default.

Prepare and students

  1. Weak spots to practice. One click from live results makes a practice quiz on the most-missed questions.
  2. A QR poster per course. Students keep one code all term and always find the latest materials.
  3. Release the answer key later. After the test is over, as its own step.
  4. Exam versions A and B. Same questions, different order and options.
  5. Keep student data small. Results belong to a session and are deleted at the end of the term unless exported. Saskatchewan school divisions fall under LAFOIP, so check with your division before storing student numbers.

Questions for you

Each has the default I will use if you say “go with your suggestions”.

Q1Timer: shared or per student?

You described the timer starting when each student joins. Default: one shared clock the teacher starts once the lobby looks full; late joiners get the time left. With a clock per student, early finishers can pass answers to late joiners, and the reveal has to wait for the last person who joined.

Q2What do students type to join?

Default: name required; student number on or off per session, on by default. Some classes will not want student numbers on a new website.

Q3Exam merged with quiz?

Default: one builder driven by templates. Quiz, Test and Exam stay as chips; built-in templates are Quiz, Unit test, Midterm and Final. The code already treats them as the same thing with different defaults.

Q4Who can open a course page?

Default: anyone with the link or QR (unlisted, not searchable). The teacher can reset the link. The alternative is a class code students type, which is safer but adds a step.

Q5Which extra types stay?

Default: keep Lesson plan (under More) and Practice (worksheet and homework). Drop Interactive graph.

Q6Live question types

Default: multiple choice, true or false, number, short answer. Matching, long answers and image questions are awkward on a phone under a timer.

Q7Per-student explanations at the reveal?

Default: no. One class-level explanation per question on the projector; personal explanations later. A personal explanation for every student is one AI call each, which matters for cost and rate limits.

Q8Who sees a teacher’s templates?

Default: only that teacher for now; sharing with a department comes after accounts exist. Departments often share one exam format, so sharing is likely the next request.

Q9Does a template keep the cover page and instructions?

Default: yes. School name, exam rules, formula sheet, and where students write their name and number.

Q10When may the bank repeat a question?

Default: a question used with the same course in the last 60 days is skipped unless it is pinned. Students in the same class compare papers; next year’s class does not see last year’s exam.

After this

Once the flows are agreed: the UI (screen by screen, starting with Home, the course page, the Make box and the live lobby), then the architecture. Live mode is what drives the architecture: it needs a server, a database and a real-time channel, and none of the three exist today. AI keys, per-teacher limits and rate limiting belong to that phase too.
